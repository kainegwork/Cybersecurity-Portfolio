# Ticket Triage and Prioritisation

## Overview

When deciding how urgent a ticket is, I would focus mainly on its effect on the business and how quickly it needs attention. An issue that is frustrating for one person may not be as urgent as a problem affecting an entire department.

## Factors I Would Consider

### Impact

First, I would establish how many people or business functions are affected:

* One user
* Several users
* A whole department
* The wider organisation

### Urgency

I would then consider how quickly the issue needs to be resolved. This could depend on:

* Upcoming business deadlines
* Whether a critical system is affected
* Any security concerns
* The amount of productivity being lost
* Whether users have a suitable workaround

### Security

Security incidents may need to be escalated immediately, even if only one user is affected.

Examples include:

* A potentially compromised account
* Malware being detected
* A suspected phishing attack
* A lost device containing sensitive data
* Unauthorised access to systems or information

## Example Priorities

### P1 — Critical

**Example:** The organisation's network is unavailable.

**Impact:** Staff across the organisation are unable to work normally.

**Action:** Begin investigating immediately and escalate to the appropriate technical teams or suppliers.

### P2 — High

**Example:** The Finance department cannot access an essential business application.

**Impact:** A key business function is unavailable to several users.

**Action:** Prioritise the investigation, provide regular updates, and keep the affected users informed.

### P3 — Medium

**Example:** One user is unable to print.

**Impact:** The issue affects a single user, and another printer or workaround may be available.

**Action:** Investigate it in line with the normal support queue and agreed service levels.

### P4 — Low

**Example:** A user requests the installation of a non-essential application.

**Impact:** There is little or no immediate effect on business operations.

**Action:** Arrange the request around the existing workload and follow any required approval process.

## Important Principle

I would use the organisation's agreed priority definitions, service levels, and escalation procedures when categorising tickets. Personal judgement is useful, but it should support the established process rather than replace it.
